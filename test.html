Setting Out on the Journey – January 1969

My journey to Tharaka Harambee Secondary School officially began on 6th January 1969. It was a day filled with anticipation, uncertainty, and a quiet sense of determination shared by all of us who were set to begin this new chapter.

Illustration: Students gathering at Chiakanga Market with their belongings

Our group converged at Chiakanga Market, a familiar point from which we would start our long trip to Marimanti. Since we were all unfamiliar with the region ahead of us, the group decided that it would be wise to spend the night at Chiakanga before continuing.

"That evening, we rested together, discussing our expectations and silently preparing ourselves for the unknown challenges of secondary school life."

On the morning of 7th January 1969, we resumed our journey and finally arrived at Marimanti, the location of Tharaka Harambee Secondary School. Accommodation was scarce, and our first night was a true introduction to the hardships we would soon experience.

We slept on the floor of a small single room, laying palm leaves to serve as mats. Despite the discomfort, we remained hopeful and united. Those present that night included Kiania, Mitugo, Kennedy, Murauko, and Francis Nyamu Kagwima, all sharing in the same mixture of excitement and uncertainty.

Journey to the School and Registration

The next day, Tuesday 7th January 1969, we walked to the school with our belongings balanced on our heads and shoulders. Our luggage mainly consisted of wooden boxes containing our clothes, bedding, and personal items.

Students carrying wooden boxes with their belongings on their heads

Upon reaching Marimanti, we gathered at the Methodist Church, which served as the temporary administrative centre for registration.

We were warmly received by the Head Teacher, Mr. Josphat Kinyua Migwi, a disciplined and focused leader who guided us through the registration procedures. Once registered, we were instructed to locate temporary accommodation as we prepared for the start of lessons.

"By good fortune, we were granted permission to board at Kamatungu Primary School. Although the boarding facilities were modest, we were grateful to sleep in the dormitory alongside the primary school pupils, giving us a temporary sense of stability."

This temporary arrangement provided a brief respite as we prepared ourselves for the academic challenges ahead, unaware of the additional trials that awaited us in terms of basic facilities and daily survival.

Early School Life and the Challenges We Faced

Limited Facilities and Harsh Living Conditions

School life in those early days was marked by numerous hardships. Basic facilities were minimal, and almost every aspect of life required personal initiative.

1969

The year of humble beginnings at Tharaka Harambee Secondary School

Although the school provided lighting for evening studies, other essential needs—such as proper accommodation and cooking areas—were lacking. The struggle to balance academic expectations with daily survival became a defining part of our experience.

Students studying by lamplight in the evenings

In response to the difficult living conditions, about five of us made a collective decision to construct our own makeshift shelter. This decision marked the beginning of an extraordinary exercise in self-reliance and cooperation that would bond us for life.

Building Our Own Temporary Shelter

The area that is now part of the school compound was then covered in thick bush and undeveloped land. We chose a small, secluded section within that bush where we would build our temporary hut for cooking, sleeping, and storing our belongings.

Students cutting wooden poles and gathering building materials

The construction process demanded strength, creativity, and patience. We spent two full months cutting wooden poles, gathering long grass, and collecting other building materials from the surrounding environment.

Once the framework was ready, we used two weeks to smear mud around the wooden structure, sealing the walls carefully to protect us from wind and insects. For the door, we tied together palm stalks to create a strong but simple entrance.

"All the work—labor, sourcing materials, and transportation—was entirely our own effort."

Weekends became our construction days. Every Saturday and Sunday, we walked into the nearby bushes to gather additional grass, wood, and mud, and then carried these materials back to the building site.

Once the hut was complete, we even installed shelves made from wooden planks, giving it a sense of order and comfort. At night, wildlife such as hippos occasionally approached our dwelling, and we learned to chase them away using burning firewood—a frightening but memorable part of our life there.

Students defending their shelter from wildlife with burning firewood

Development and Growth of the School (1970 and Beyond)

Arrival of American Volunteer William James

The year 1970 marked the beginning of significant transformation at Tharaka Harambee Secondary School. The arrival of an American volunteer, Mr. William James, brought renewed energy, structure, and development.

1970

Year of transformation with the arrival of Mr. William James

His influence was immediately visible. Under his leadership, the thick bush surrounding the school was cleared rapidly, opening space for future development and creating a safer environment.

Mr. James taught subjects such as English and History. He recognized potential in many of us and encouraged discipline and hard work. It was through his guidance that I was appointed school librarian, with Kanyamba Ruka serving as my assistant—a role that taught me responsibility and organization.

"During exams (KJSE) over 77% of the students passed in the two subjects he taught."

Growth in Infrastructure and School Facilities

During the same period, the school began offering lunch, although it was served in the open yard since there was no dining hall. Despite the lack of proper facilities, morale remained strong, and students remained committed to their studies.

The Methodist Church played a crucial role in the school's existence by offering initial classrooms. Classroom construction began, and Mr. William James ensured that the school was registered as an examination center.

First classrooms at Tharaka Harambee Secondary School

He further persuaded the government to take over Tharaka Harambee Secondary School from the parents and have it recognized as a government-registered school.

Unfortunately, the heavy workload frequently made him sick, forcing him to return to America for treatment and to stay away from mosquitoes, which were causing recurrent malaria attacks.

Before he left, he made sure arrangements were in place for us to continue our education in other well-established secondary schools.

As the school continued developing, it eventually achieved registration as an examination center for the Kenya Junior Secondary Education (KJSE), marking an important milestone in its path toward becoming a fully established institution.

Academic Motivation and My Career Path

Influence of Dedicated Teachers

My academic growth was shaped significantly by the encouragement and mentorship of Mr. William James, whose teaching style inspired curiosity and discipline. Through his support, many students excelled in their subjects, particularly in science and practical work.

"I remain proud to have been among those who performed well under his guidance."

Progression to Higher Education and Engineering Career

Following my success at Tharaka Harambee Secondary School, I earned admission to Nkubu High School, where I continued to excel academically, especially in science-related subjects.

Academic progression from Tharaka Harambee to Nkubu High School

My educational journey eventually led me into the engineering field. Becoming the 1st engineer from the Tharaka community stands as one of my greatest achievements, enabling me to contribute to the development of the very society that shaped my early life and values.

First Engineer

The resilience and problem-solving skills developed during those challenging early years at Tharaka Harambee Secondary School became foundational to my success in engineering and my approach to life's challenges.

Conclusion

This narrative captures the humble yet powerful beginnings of my educational journey and the early development of Tharaka Harambee Secondary School.

"From travelling long distances and sleeping on palm leaves, to constructing our own shelter and studying under challenging conditions, every experience contributed to shaping our resilience and determination."

The arrival of dedicated individuals, such as Mr. William James, and the support of the Methodist Church fueled the transformation of the school from a bushy compound to a growing educational institution.

The growth of Tharaka Harambee Secondary School from humble beginnings

Ultimately, these formative years laid the foundation for my academic achievement and career in engineering. The journey stands as a lasting testament to perseverance, community spirit, and the transformative power of education.
